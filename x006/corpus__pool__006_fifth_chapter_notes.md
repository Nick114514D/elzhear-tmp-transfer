# 006 — *Requiem* — editing map
_four movements · ~14:07 · C♯ throughout, bent up to D at the close · 11-limit just intonation (Partch) crossed with Messiaen colour · Penderecki's sonorist mass · Mozart's Requiem returning as rotting tape · the two Verklärungen braided · opening on the dominant 005 leaves_

The fifth chapter is the requiem proper, and it takes the Mass for the dead as its architecture. Four movements track the liturgy — *Requiem aeternam*, *Pie Jesu*, *Dies irae*, *Lux aeterna* — with one displacement that decides the whole piece: the **Pie Jesu is moved forward**, placed second, ahead of the terror instead of at the end of the sequence where the rite puts it. The plea is spoken before the wrath arrives. It goes unanswered, and the Dies irae swallows it. The light that returns at the end arrives over a piece that stopped asking.

**The seam from 005.** *Syncope* flatlines and leaves F♯ minor. 006 opens on its dominant, **C♯**, and holds that pitch as the spine of the entire work: the bell theme tolls C♯5–D♯5–D5, the exact three pitches 005's residue carries; each of the first three movements ends on a bare C♯; and the assembled piece analyses with C♯ as its dominant chroma. The pitch is the corpse the requiem is sung over.

**The tuning.** Movements I and II are voiced in **11-limit just intonation** after Partch — a 105 Hz base, otonality (1/1, 9/8, 5/4, 11/8, 3/2, 7/4) set against utonality (1/1, 8/7, 4/3, 16/11, 8/5, 16/9), with syntonic and septimal commas (81/80, 64/63) detuning the clusters. The convergence that makes this work: the augmented fourth Messiaen builds his colour-chords from is the **11/8**, and his shimmering coloristic seventh sits close to the **7/4**. Messiaen's harmony realized in Partch's tuning comes out purer and stranger than equal temperament allows. Utonal clusters carry the dark stations; one station only is otonal.

**The mass and the band.** Penderecki supplies what tuning cannot: the *Threnody* apparatus — an atonal mass churning underneath, a high band of twenty-two voices shrieking above, tonality submerged and resurfacing. Movement I alternates them station by station. Movement III turns the same apparatus on the Mozart tapes.

**The ghosts.** Movement III plays Mozart's Requiem as a failing cassette — *Rex tremendae*, *Lacrimosa*, and a harpsichord *Dies irae*, each through the Christopherson treatment (tape-warp wow and flutter, saturation, bandpass, bitcrush, granular scatter) and re-pitched onto C♯ and G. The dead composer's mass for the dead, played back on rotting tape.

**The two Verklärungen.** Movement IV braids Schoenberg's transfiguration from *Verklärte Nacht* (1899) with Strauss's from *Tod und Verklärung* (1889), both realized in-engine from public-domain scores, and lets the C♯ bend up a semitone into **D**. Nine broad D-major chords in the *Sehr breit und langsam* language, a rising tender line, a D6/9 apotheosis. The album's accumulated violence resolved into light and handed forward.

## Movement I — Lament (*Requiem aeternam*) — 3:20
_assembled 0:00 · 58 bpm, bar 4.14 s · C♯ · arch form over eight Partch stations_

Three strata run the length of the movement: the **tonal anchors** (struck just-intoned Karplus clusters, a rolling arpeggiated protagonist, a singing solo lament line, a grave voice-choir), the **atonal mass**, and the high **threnody band**. The arch alternates them — passes where the lament is legible, episodes where it drowns.

The chaconne is eight stations descending: **C♯3 – B2 – A2 – G♯2 – F♯2 – E3 – D♯4 – C♯3**, each with its own ratios (station 1: 1, 6/5, 3/2, 7/4; station 3 opens the 11/8). Station 6 (E) is the single **otonal** station, the one place the harmony opens upward. Station 7 is the **D♯ choke** — a bare 16/15 semitone dyad thrown into the high register, the climax of the movement. Station 8 returns to C♯ as a minor triad with the octave: the partial resolve.

The voice layer is a LibriVox reading of Irenaeus's *Demonstration of the Apostolic Preaching*, corroded into a grave choir. The **bell theme** — C♯5–D♯5–D5 — is a quiet recurring three-note figure, blended into the reverb instead of tolling above it, returning at the intro, both arch seams, and the coda.

### 1.  0:00 — Intro
- **Harmony:** bare C♯. The mass emerges out of 005's silence; a bell and a C♯ anchor set the pitch. The bell theme states itself for the first time.

### 2.  0:12 — Pass 1
- **Harmony:** the eight stations stated plainly. Anchors on every station, the mass thin and ducking under each strike, no band. The chaconne legible.

### 3.  0:45 — Arche I
- **Harmony:** submerged. Anchors fall silent, the atonal mass swells, the threnody band enters above. The bell theme marks the seam.

### 4.  1:13 — Pass 2
- **Harmony:** the stations again, fuller. Anchors re-emerge with a thicker voice-choir, fighting up through a heavier mass; the band stays present; the solo lament line grows.

### 5.  1:46 — Arche II
- **Harmony:** submerged, deeper. The largest atonal swell, the band climbing — the most violent drowning of the chaconne. Bell theme at the seam.

### 6.  2:19 — Pass 3
- **Harmony:** the stations forced through dense mass and band. The **D♯ choke** at station 7 is the climax, a high shriek tearing across it. Station 8 begins the partial resolve.

### 7.  2:52 — Coda
- **Harmony:** C♯, half-resolved. Mass and band recede; a final C♯ anchor and the bell theme in the wreckage, fading. Residue: bare C♯.

## Movement II — Pie Jesu (the hinge) — 2:47
_assembled 3:20 · E-centred, the C♯ absorbed as its sixth · Messiaen colour realized in 11-limit just intonation_

The plea, placed ahead of the terror. Movement I ends on bare C♯; the first chord here is **E6/9**, in which C♯ is the sixth — the lament's grief-tonic taken up as a colour-tone the instant the E pedal enters. The home of the grief becomes a glint inside the light.

The progression is Messiaen's colour-harmony, tuned true:

    E6/9 · F♯7sus♭9/E · B13/E · C♯m(add♭6)/E · Amaj7♯11/E · Fmaj7♯11/E · G♯7♭9 · E6/9

Every ♯11 in it is the 11/8 the clusters of movement I were built from. The lineage runs *Fête des belles eaux* → "Louange à l'Immortalité de Jésus" → 001 *Cire I* → here: the prayer at the centre of the requiem echoing the prayer that opened the album, the record folding back on itself at its most tender point. The kinship is left unquoted — no callback line, no long stretch of stasis. The threat is audible the whole way through.

### 1.  0:00 — Intro
- **Harmony:** C♯4 carried across from movement I as a swell, FM bells thickening over it. The pitch is handed forward before the key declares itself.

### 2.  ~0:13 — Session 1 — the Duruflé dream
- **Harmony:** two cycles of the colour-progression, ondes-like voices swelling through it. Tenderness with the threat mass already faint underneath.

### 3.  — Glue — the harp steals in
- **Harmony:** the same colours, E-pedalled. A harp enters over the receding dream; the two sessions overlap as layers instead of handing off.

### 4.  — Session 2 — Pendereckian *Pagodes*
- **Harmony:** the progression again under Debussy's arabesque (harp, Karplus, marimba), gathering. The threat mass rises through it toward the fracture.

### 5.  — Transition — the brightness sours
- **Harmony:** E detuning into atonality. A shivering cloud centred on E widens across the last two arabesque spans, the *Pagodes* brightness darkening from inside.

### 6.  — Outro (30 s)
- **Harmony:** dissolved. An atonal cloud decaying, metal-brass, timpani, tape-rewind — the dream taken apart on its own tape. Residue: the plea unanswered.

## Movement III — Dies irae — 4:38
_assembled 6:10 · C♯ / G · Mozart's Requiem as rotting cassette · the sonorist apparatus turned on the tape_

The terror, and the longest movement. The Mozart ghosts arrive under full Christopherson treatment, re-pitched onto the C♯/G axis so the borrowed music sits inside the album's own pitch world.

### 1.  0:00 — Dies irae build
- **Harmony:** C♯, gathering. The wall assembles from below; the Mozart ghosts foreshadowed faintly, still unreadable.

### 2.  1:02 — Rex climax
- **Harmony:** the *Rex tremendae* roar, twice. **Open breaks** — true silence between the cries, so the roar lands clear each time.

### 3.  1:48 — Subside
- **Harmony:** the wall decaying into the groove that follows.

### 4.  2:00 — Solar Lodge
- **Harmony:** a prolonged merciless groove — industrial kick, backbeat clank, downbeat strike, piston — peaking and decaying. The *Lacrimosa* ghost weeps corroded over the machine; a keening reed line (the Thrower clarinet nod); the harpsichord *Dies irae* driving underneath; a "black sun" surge.

### 5.  3:25 — Confutatis
- **Harmony:** descending. The damned voice-wall and the flames (a descending threnody) with the harpsichord, faded in out of the Solar Lodge decay as one continuous descent, no volume step.

### 6.  — Outro — the broken cassette
- **Harmony:** the descending line D–C–B♭–A–G–F♯–E♭–D after the *Rex* cries, on Karplus with an off-key harpsichord companion, played through a stuck/rewind-whirr/resume tape effect on grid-aligned cuts. The Solar Lodge metal percussion returns for the last ~30 s and fades.

### 7.  4:08 — Collapse
- **Harmony:** bare C♯. No strikes — the C♯ sub alone and a final *Lacrimosa* ghost trace, 6 s master fade. Residue: the same C♯ that opened the piece, stripped to nothing.

## Movement IV — Lux aeterna — 3:26
_assembled 10:42, attacca · C♯ bent up to D · the two Verklärungen braided · closing on 1925 shellac_

The coda. Movement III's bare C♯2 thins to a thread and never reaches silence; movement IV's C♯3 blooms out of it an octave up — the residue reborn as light — and then bends up a semitone into D.

This movement exists as an **audio-domain recomposition**: its r5 synthesis script was lost before it could be committed, so `mvt4_LOCKED.py` operates on the r5 render itself. The chain below is what that script performs.

### 1.  0:10 — Opening — the C♯→D lift
- **Harmony:** C♯ lifting to D over the *Verklärte Nacht* bed. At **0:07** the C♯ residue tone (~141 Hz C♯3 with harmonics at 282 and 423) is given a defined fundamental, its sub-mush high-passed, its reedy third harmonic and nasal 900 Hz cut, and pushed distant through a 3 s room — concrete and far off, without boom or shrillness.
- Two faint focused-grain blooms of the Beethoven Op. 135 *Lento assai* theme, pitched to D, are merged in at **0:16** and **0:33** at −25 dBFS.

### 2.  0:50 — The D♭ Ruhegesang curtain
- **Harmony:** D♭, broad and slow. At **1:21** the major-string phrase is de-boxed (its energy sat 72% in 300–600 Hz) and its thin 7.5–11 kHz edge shelved down, lightening the weight.

### 3.  1:54 — The seam
- **Harmony:** the trough between paragraphs. The Op. 135 theme's final cadential phrase (D♭, aged, −21 dBFS) is placed to land at **2:01.5**, exactly where the post-gap material rises back into the returning theme — the recording glues the seam instead of replaying over it.

### 4.  2:02 — The rise — Strauss's transfiguration
- **Harmony:** D♭ lifting to D. Strauss's seven-note transfiguration theme from *Tod und Verklärung*, realized in-engine, braided with Schoenberg's — the two Verklärungen sounding as one. Nine broad D-major chords, a rising line, a D6/9 apotheosis. Left clean, no sample.

### 5.  2:50 — Coda — the 1925 runout
- **Harmony:** D, settled. The shellac runout closes the piece. Residue handed to 007: **D**, and the surface noise of a 1925 recording.

## Assembly
`sandbox/006/assemble_full.py` (commit `4d01a15b`) joins the four masters. Per-movement mastered levels are preserved — the quiet-I to present-IV arc is intentional. Output ~14:07, peak −0.80 dBFS.

- **Inter-movement air.** Dead digital silence between movements reads as a vacuum: movement I decays to its own ~−58 dB room floor, and an absolute-zero gap drops that ever-present air to nothing. A low tape-room **floor bed** (decorrelated stereo band-limited hiss, 90–9000 Hz, plus a touch of 40–300 Hz room tone, at ~−56 dB RMS) bridges each inner breath so the air never dies.
- **I → II (3:18–3:28).** The floor holds after movement I; movement II is eased in on a two-stage envelope as a quiet **intro plateau** (~−47 dB) that co-sounds with the floor as two audible layers. The floor fades out at ~3:25.5, and the harmony swells up only after it has gone, arriving full at **3:28**. The order is the point: breath and intro together → breath clears → harmony rises.
- **II → III.** A short (~3 s) floored breath, same bed.
- **III → IV.** **Attacca**, 4 s equal-power crossfade. Movement III's low C♯2 thins to a thread and never reaches silence; movement IV's C♯3 blooms out of it. No silent gap.

**Sources drawn on:** Harry Partch (11-limit just intonation, otonality and utonality); Penderecki, *Threnody for the Victims of Hiroshima* and the *St Luke Passion* (the atonal mass, the high band, the choir-cloud); Messiaen, *Fête des belles eaux* and "Louange à l'Immortalité de Jésus" from the *Quatuor pour la fin du temps* (the colour-chords, the ondes voice, the lineage back to 001); Duruflé, *Requiem* (the dream); Debussy, "Pagodes" from *Estampes* (the arabesque of movement II); Mozart, *Requiem* K. 626 — *Rex tremendae*, *Lacrimosa*, and a harpsichord arrangement of the *Dies irae* (the ghosts of movement III); Peter Christopherson and Coil (the tape treatment, the Solar Lodge groove, the keening clarinet after Thrower); Schoenberg, *Verklärte Nacht* (1899); Richard Strauss, *Tod und Verklärung*, Op. 24 (1889); Beethoven, String Quartet No. 16 in F, Op. 135, III *Lento assai* (the granular source of movement IV); Irenaeus, *Demonstration of the Apostolic Preaching* (the voice layer of movement I). The opening C♯ recovers the close of 005, *Syncope*. Engine: the custom Python/NumPy synthesis, no DAW.

**On the title.** *Requiem* is taken plainly, as the Mass itself: the album reaches the point where it has to bury its own dead, and the liturgy supplies the four-movement architecture. The one liberty is the order — the *Pie Jesu*, which the rite places at the close of the Dies irae sequence, is moved ahead of it, so the plea is offered before the terror and is left standing when the terror answers.
